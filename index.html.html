Foundations

Antibodies & Epitopes

Antibodies

Immunoglobulins produced by B cells. They recognise and bind antigens with high specificity.


Epitopes

The specific molecular region of an antigen that an antibody binds directly.


Polyclonal vs monoclonal

Polyclonal antibodies target many epitopes; monoclonal antibodies target one.

Comparison

Polyclonal or Monoclonal?

Polyclonal

Arises from many B-cell clones; binds many epitopes; supply limited by the donor animal.

VS

Monoclonal

Arises from one identical hybridoma clone; targets a single epitope; produced continuously.

The Principle

How the Hybridoma Works

Developed in 1975 by George Köhler and César Milstein.

B cell

Myeloma cell

=

Hybridoma

Combines antibody production with cellular immortality.

Production Workflow

Immunisation, Fusion & Cloning

1

1. Immunise

The animal is immunised with the antigen, then activated B cells are isolated from the spleen.

2

2. Fuse

Spleen cells are fused with HGPRT-deficient myeloma cells using PEG or electrical fusion.

3

3. Screen & clone

ELISA identifies producing cells, then one hybridoma is cloned by limiting dilution.

Selection

Selecting Hybridomas with HAT Medium

Hypoxanthine, Aminopterin, Thymidine.

✕

Unfused B cells

They carry HGPRT but die naturally within days.

✕

Unfused myeloma

Aminopterin blocks DNA synthesis and they lack HGPRT, so they die.

✓

Hybridoma cells

They inherit HGPRT from the B cell and immortality from the myeloma, so they survive.

Scale-up

Large-Scale Production & Purification

01Scale-up

Selected clones are expanded in bioreactors to raise cell density and antibody yield.

02Clarify

Centrifugation and filtration separate the supernatant from cell debris.

03Capture

Protein A or Fc-directed columns bind the antibody.

04Quality control

Purity, sterility, absence of endotoxins and target binding are confirmed.

Evolution

Evolution & Humanization

MurineHuman

Murine (omab)

Fully murine protein: higher risk of an immune response and faster clearance.

Chimeric (ximab)

About 33% murine variable regions and about 67% human constant regions.

Humanized (zumab)

Over 90% human sequence; only the CDR binding loops remain murine.

Fully human (umab)

Entirely human sequence with low immunogenicity, made in engineered cell lines.

Applications

Therapeutic & Diagnostic Uses

Cancer

Targeting HER2 or CD20, and checkpoint inhibition to boost anti-tumour immunity.

Immune disorders

Neutralising TNF-α in rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis and Crohn’s disease.

Infectious disease

Passive immunity directed against viral pathogens and bacterial toxins.

Diagnostics

Pregnancy tests, Western blot, ELISA, flow cytometry and immunohistochemistry.

Conclusion

Today’s Challenges & Tomorrow’s Innovations

Monoclonal antibodies have transformed medicine by targeting disease mechanisms with precision and reproducibility.

Challenges

High cost of development and manufacturing; complex cold-chain storage; immune-related adverse effects.

Outlook

Antibody–drug conjugates; bispecific antibodies; AI-guided antibody engineering.
